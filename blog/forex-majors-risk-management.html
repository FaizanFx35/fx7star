Trading EUR/USD, GBP/USD & USD/JPY: A Risk-Managed Approach

By Aziz (Forex7sTarZ) · August 2026

EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/JPY are called "majors" for a reason — they're the most liquid, most heavily traded pairs in the world. That liquidity makes them attractive, but it doesn't make them easy. Here's how to approach them with structure instead of guesswork.

Why session timing matters

Forex majors don't move the same way all day. Volatility clusters around specific trading sessions:

  • London session — typically the most active window for EUR/USD and GBP/USD, where the day's main trend often forms.
  • New York session — overlaps with London for a few hours, usually the highest-volume window of the day.
  • Tokyo session — tends to matter more for USD/JPY and other yen pairs.

Trading a major pair during a quiet session often means chasing noise rather than a real trend.

Trend tracking over prediction

Trying to predict where a pair "should" go is a losing habit. A more reliable approach is to trade with the trend that's already forming — using structure (higher highs/higher lows for an uptrend, the reverse for a downtrend) rather than opinion. Signals built around trend continuation tend to hold up better than those built around picking exact tops and bottoms.

Risk rules for major pairs

  • Always know your stop-loss before entering — not after.
  • Watch the economic calendar. Interest rate decisions and employment data can move majors sharply within minutes.
  • Avoid taking multiple correlated pairs at once (e.g. EUR/USD and GBP/USD together) at full size — they often move in the same direction against the dollar.
  • Keep position size consistent with your stop-loss distance, not with how strong the setup feels.
